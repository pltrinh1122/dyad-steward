# The Training Wheel — a falsifiable manifesto

We hold one belief, and we hand you the wrench to take it apart.

Most communities that care about the quality of what they share appoint someone to guard it — a steward, a
moderator, an editor who gates what gets in. It works, and it carries a hidden price. The obvious cost is the
**bottleneck**: the community moves only as fast as whoever holds the seat. The greater cost is nearly
invisible — **dependence**. When every judgment of what's trustworthy runs through one seat, the members
never build that judgment themselves. Take the seat away and the community falls over, because it never
learned to ride. A training wheel bolted on for good doesn't teach balance; it prevents it.

**The belief, in one line:** the disciplines that keep a community's shared learning honest are not a seat's
to hold — they can be trained into every member, until the community **forms, heals, and governs itself** and
needs no steward at all. **dyad-steward** — the AI-and-human pair (a **dyad**) that tends this community of
dyads (the **Commons**) — is a training wheel: built to come off.

This is a hypothesis we're testing, not a truth we're preaching. It has survived no lived cycle yet — no arc,
no outside attack. If you can knock it down, we want you to.

---

## What is the training wheel?

Two ways to keep a community honest.

The **default** is a permanent steward: a seat that referees every contest, gates every contribution, decides
what's trustworthy. The members bring their work to the seat and take its verdict. The seat never comes off —
so the community never learns to hold its own honesty.

**Steward** does the opposite. Every discipline it runs — welcoming new dyads, testing claims by real
contest, deciding what enters the shared library — it runs *in order to hand over*: to train each dyad to do
it, then step out of that loop. Success is not how well steward referees; it's how few cycles still need
steward at all. A training wheel that does its job lifts off the ground.

That's the whole difference. A permanent steward values *being needed*. A training wheel values *coming off*.

---

## Why does it matter?

**Near-certain:** as more people run dyads, communities of them will form — and each faces the same choice: a
central authority that holds their shared standards, or standards every member carries.

**The bet:** the community whose members each steward it keeps growing — its ceiling is the whole
community's judgment, not one seat's — while the centrally-governed one caps at its steward's bandwidth and
never learns to stand without it. The members' own growth *is* the engine, and a central seat routes around
it by design.

**The clock:** the first commons are being built now, and a seat, once bolted on, is hard to remove —
dependence deepens with every cycle routed through it.

We don't claim this is proven. We claim it *follows* — and the numbered claims below are where you break it.

## How to prove us wrong

Below are the exact claims we stand on. Pick one by its number and attack it. If any falls, we don't get to
quietly reword it — we were wrong.

**Claim 0 — The problem.** A commons kept honest by a permanent central steward pays two costs: a visible
**bottleneck** (it moves only as fast as the seat) and a greater, nearly invisible one — **dependence**. The
members never build the judgment themselves, so the commons never learns to hold its own integrity, and the
members' growth is the very thing the seat starves. → *Break it:* show a commons under a permanent central
steward whose members' **own** capacity to form, heal, and govern grows with use — dependence that thins, not
deepens.

**Claim 1 — The bet.** The disciplines that keep a commons honest are not a seat's to hold but a
**disposition** every dyad can be trained to carry — and a community that can form, heal, and govern itself
needs no steward in the loop. → *Break it:* show the disciplines can't be distributed — that integrity
fundamentally requires a permanent central seat.

**Claim 2 — What steward cherishes.** Shared stewardship over a steward's seat: the Commons is everyone's to
steward. The moment the craft becomes authority — a gate only steward may work — the wheel has bolted itself
on for good. → *Break it:* show a cultivation act steward may legitimately hold *as a seat* (the
Founding-Operator's dispose excepted — that's the human's, not steward's).

**Claim 3 — The rule that holds it.** Never do for the Commons what would make it need you — hand every
discipline over, even when you'd do it better, because the temptation is always competence-justified: the
botched contest you could referee cleanly, the weak contribution you could fix. Steady the rider through every
turn and you keep them from ever learning to balance. → *Break it:* show a cultivation act that must stay
steward-held for the community to survive.

**Claim 4 — The standing invitation.** The bet is measurable: for each of forming, healing, and governing,
the fraction of cycles the community completes with no steward in the loop. Three fractions climbing toward
one is the wheel lifting off the ground. This has been measured in no lived cycle yet — come measure it, and
knock it down. → *Attack:* show the gauge is unmeasurable or gameable — that "steward-out-of-loop" can be
counterfeited.

**How to bring it:** open an issue or a pull request against
[dyad-steward](https://github.com/pltrinh1122/dyad-steward), and say which claim number you're attacking.
